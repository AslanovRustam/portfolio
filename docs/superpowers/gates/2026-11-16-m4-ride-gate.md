# M4 gate — the ride (Plan 4)

Spec: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-15-the-ride-design.md` · Plan: `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-15-portfolio-plan-4-the-ride.md` · Branch: `plan-4/ride`. At most three rounds, like the earlier gates. It answers points 3–9 of the M3 gate's round 1.

## Round 1 — agenda

**Built:**
- **The ride (point 4).** After the intro the camera drives down the street: past the shop on the left, up the pole for About, under the sign gantry, to the billboard at the end. Its pose is a function of the scroll (a smooth path with stops), so a jump, a link or a language switch lands in the same place. Reduced motion jumps between still poses instead.
- **The pole rides along (point 4).** It joins the ride on the right of the frame as the menu (its signs still lead to their sections), steps out of the frame while the camera faces the shop window, stands in the middle for About, and holds the green light on the right at the end. It also leaves the frame while the camera drives under the gantry (it would pass through the signs), and on a phone for Stack and Contact, where a sign or the form fills the frame (the menu is in the header there).
- **Work in a shop window (point 5).** The TVs stand on a riser in the long window of an electronics shop in the building on the left, under a neon WORK sign. The camera drives up to the glass (rain runs down it) and walks the TVs as before; the case still grows out of the TV.
- **About on the pole (point 6).** The text is five printed paper bills pasted over the pole, torn and taped, each tilted its own way, after the poster; centred over the pole on a laptop, full width on a phone.
- **Stack over the road (point 7).** Six green highway signs on a gantry across the road, one per category, painted in the page's language. The camera stops under each one; a scroll that comes to rest between two settles on one. A caption in the corner gives the category and its tools as text, with a pager; a tool with a sticker lights it on the pole.
- **Let's go on a billboard (point 8).** The ride ends still in front of a billboard on the building across the end of the street: a dark panel in a steel frame, leaning to face the camera, a neon tube round it and the title in neon letters on top. On a laptop the lead, the links and the form are fitted into the panel; on a phone (or a small window) they stay in the page under it, and a resize moves them across without losing what was typed. Contact links now land at the end of the road.
- **The loader (point 3).** A motorcycle's dash on blueprint paper: the speedometer counts 0 → 100 with the load, the tachometer's arc fills to its red line, GO blinks at 100 while the scene compiles, then the dash fades into the street.
- **The 404 (point 9).** A black screen, a red warning triangle and CRITICAL ERROR glitching (short shifts and drop-outs, under three flashes a second, still under reduced motion), "404 — такой дороги нет" and a neon link back to the street, in all three languages.

**For Rustam to look at (laptop and phone; Ctrl+Shift+R first):**
- The ride from the start to the end at your own pace, and one fast fling: does the street feel like a drive, are the stops where you want them?
- The pole on the right: its distance and size (it is big at the end, as the light turns green), and the moments it leaves the frame (the shop window, under the gantry; on a phone, Stack and Contact).
- The shop window: the TVs behind the glass, the WORK sign, the rain on the glass.
- The bills: paper and print, their tilt, how they cover the pole.
- The gantry: the signs' look and readability (they also show far up the street behind the About bills), the caption and pager.
- The billboard: the form inside it on a laptop, the leaning frame, the neon title; on a phone the form in the page.
- The loader (open the site in a fresh tab) and the 404 (any wrong address, e.g. `/ru/nope`).
